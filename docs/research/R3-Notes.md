## Week3 Research

This week I watched a video about how to use pages in Figma and learned why having multiple pages is important for staying organized. The video explained that instead of putting all your designs on one huge canvas, you can separate them into different pages for things like rough drafts. Also learned that using multiple pages is a good design habit because it helps you find your work faster and makes it easier to fix mistakes later.

This week I did the reading opn UX and design and learned how small interface choices can either help users or quietly push them into decisions they did not really want, like the dark design patterns the UX Tigers post calls out. UX laws like Choice Overload and Hick’s Law, helped understand why keeping options simple and familiar makes people move faster and feel less stressed when making descisions. On the visual side,  a clear focal point, and negative space, plus sticking to a grid can make designs not feel messy. The Last thing was accessibility basics, the web apps work best when you do not reinvent patterns, you group related elements, and you keep features focused.

This week I read Brad Frost’s “Atomic Design” article and learned how design systems can be built in a more structured way by breaking interfaces into smaller reusable parts. The main idea is to build up from simple pieces and turn them into bigger components, then combine those into full layouts and pages. Frost explains this as five levels: atoms, molecules, organisms, templates, and pages, which helps teams stay consistent and scalable as projects grow.
